Why fractional

You need 10 hours a week of the right CTO.

Here's a trap I see often. A founder raises a seed round, feels the pressure to "look like a real company," and hires a senior full-time CTO at a six-figure salary plus equity. Six months later they've burned a chunk of runway on someone spending most of their time on problems a mid-level engineer could handle, because there simply isn't enough CTO-level work yet.

A fractional CTO earns its cost at specific moments:

  • Architecture decisions that are painful to reverse. A wrong call on your data model or infrastructure at month three costs you a rewrite at month eighteen.
  • Hiring your first engineers. Who to hire, in what order, where (including LATAM), and how to tell a strong candidate from a good interviewer.
  • Enterprise deals and security reviews. SOC 2, security questionnaires and compliance requirements that block sales.
  • Investor conversations. Someone who can represent your technology and roadmap credibly in a fundraise or due diligence.
  • AI strategy. Moving from a pile of AI features to a coherent, governed framework that actually scales.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a fractional CTO?

A fractional CTO is an experienced Chief Technology Officer who works with your company part-time, typically a set number of hours per week, instead of as a full-time employee. You get executive-level technology leadership for architecture, hiring, security and strategy at a fraction of the cost of a full-time hire.

When does a startup need a fractional CTO instead of a full-time CTO?

Most pre-seed to Series A startups don't have 40 hours a week of CTO-level work. They need around 10 hours a week of the right CTO: someone to make architecture decisions that are hard to reverse, hire the first engineers, prepare for security reviews like SOC 2, and represent technology to investors. A fractional CTO fits until the company has enough scale to justify a full-time executive.

What is the difference between a fractional CTO and a fractional CPO?

A fractional CTO owns how the product is built: architecture, engineering team, infrastructure, security. A fractional CPO owns what gets built and why: discovery, roadmap, prioritization and product metrics. In early-stage companies the two roles overlap heavily, so I often cover both.

Can you help us hire engineers in Latin America?

Yes. I'm based in Argentina and help startups define roles, run technical interviews and onboard their first LATAM engineers, so you get time-zone-aligned talent for US teams with a leader who knows the market.

Can a fractional CTO help us pass SOC 2?

Yes. In my most recent CTO role we passed SOC 2 Type II without freezing the roadmap. The approach is to build controls into how teams already ship, so the audit trail becomes a byproduct of good engineering instead of a separate project.
