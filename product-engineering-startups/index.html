The problem

Speed now, without a rewrite later.

Early-stage companies face two expensive failure modes. Build too slowly and you run out of runway before you find product-market fit. Build carelessly and the shortcuts you took at month three turn into a rewrite at month eighteen, right when customers and investors expect you to scale.

Product engineering led by an experienced CTO avoids both. We move fast on what's easy to change, and slow down only on the few decisions that aren't: your data model, your infrastructure, your security and compliance posture, and how AI fits into the product.

  • Senior judgment on scope, so you build less and learn more.
  • Foundations ready for enterprise buyers and security reviews like SOC 2.
  • AI-native from day one, governed and secure by design.
  • Code, infrastructure and accounts that you own outright.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is product engineering?

Product engineering combines product thinking and software engineering: deciding what to build based on customer and business needs, then designing, building, testing and iterating on it. For early-stage companies it means getting from idea to a product customers pay for, without building foundations you'll need to throw away.

How long does it take to build an MVP?

It depends on scope, but the goal is to ship the smallest product that tests your riskiest assumption in weeks, not months. We start with discovery to cut scope to what matters, then build in short iterations with working software at each step.

Do you build AI features and AI-native products?

Yes. I design AI as an operating layer rather than a pile of features: a coherent, governed and secure framework built on emerging standards like the Model Context Protocol (MCP), and I use AI-native engineering workflows to ship faster.

Who owns the code and IP?

You do. The code, infrastructure and accounts live in your company's name from day one, documented so your future team can take over without a rewrite.

What happens after the MVP launches?

We can keep iterating together, help you hire your own engineering team (including in Latin America), or transition into a fractional CTO engagement while your team grows.
