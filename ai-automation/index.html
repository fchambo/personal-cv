The problem

Plenty of AI pilots. Not much less manual work.

Most companies have tried AI by now: a chatbot, a few prompts, a tool someone signed up for. Yet the team still copies data between systems, answers the same questions every day and reviews documents by hand. The pilots never reached the processes where the cost actually sits.

Automation that sticks starts from the process and the business case, not from the model. It connects to your real systems, handles the messy cases, keeps a human in the loop where mistakes are expensive, and reports on what it saved.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Which processes are good candidates for AI automation?

High-volume, repetitive work that involves reading, classifying or writing: customer support triage and replies, document intake and data extraction, back-office and after-sale operations, reconciliations, reporting, and moving information between systems. We prioritize by volume, cost, error rate and how easy the result is to verify.

How is this different from buying an AI tool?

Off-the-shelf tools rarely fit your exact process or connect to all your systems. I start from the process and the business case, use existing tools where they fit, and build only what's needed, so the automation runs inside your workflow, with your data, and under your controls.

How do you keep AI automation safe and accurate?

Every automation gets evaluation against real cases before launch, human review where mistakes are costly, audit logs, access controls and cost limits. I've run AI in production in a SOC 2 Type II company, so security and compliance are part of the design, not an afterthought.

How long until we see results?

The goal is a first automation in production within weeks, measured against a baseline we agree on up front. Once it proves its value, we expand to the next processes on the roadmap.

Do we need an engineering team?

No. I can design and build the automations end to end. If you have engineers, I work with them and leave them able to run and extend what we built.
