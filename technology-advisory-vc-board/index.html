Why it matters

Every company says it does AI. Few can say where AI is taking their industry.

I talk with at least four founders or CEOs every week. Nearly all of them describe their AI use case well: a copilot here, an agent there, a workflow that saves ten hours a week. Very few can answer the harder question investors and boards need answered: which parts of their market get commoditized next, and where can they build an edge competitors can't copy?

The same gap shows up in architecture, security and team quality. Pitch decks don't show technical debt, a fragile data model, a missing SOC 2 plan, or an engineering team that can't scale. Those are the things that turn into down rounds and rewrites.

I bring the view of someone who has done the work: led NoNighter through SOC 2 Type II, built a 90+ person technology hub at Santander reporting to the CIO, integrated two acquisitions across five countries as CTO of Homie.mx, and presented a portfolio company to investors at Collision Conference.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What does a technical due diligence cover?

A technical due diligence reviews a company's architecture and code quality, scalability, security and compliance posture, engineering team and processes, product roadmap feasibility, use of AI, and third-party and open-source risks. The output is a clear report with red flags, risks, and the investment needed to fix them.

How long does a technical due diligence take?

It depends on the deal and the company's size. A focused pre-term-sheet review can be done in days; a full diligence for a larger round or acquisition takes longer. We agree on scope and timeline before starting so it fits your deal schedule.

Why does a board need an independent technology advisor?

Most boards are strong on finance, go-to-market and operations, but technology risk, AI strategy and engineering spend are harder to evaluate. An independent technology advisor helps the board ask the right questions, interpret what management reports, and spot risks before they become expensive.

Can you support our portfolio companies after the investment?

Yes. I work with portfolio founders on architecture, AI adoption, SOC 2 readiness, hiring and engineering leadership, and can step in as a fractional CTO when a company needs more hands-on support.

Do you help with technology integration in M&A?

Yes. I advise on technology strategy during mergers and acquisitions: assessing the target's stack and team, identifying integration risks, and planning how platforms, data and teams come together after the deal. I wrote about it in Insights on Technology Strategies during an M&A.
